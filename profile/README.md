## Looking can be an active skill

Nintendo’s tips about hidden discoveries give a useful example: a captured Moe-Eye can use special glasses to reveal hidden platforms. It is a small illustration of a larger idea. The answer can depend on changing how a place is seen, not only on making a more difficult jump.

I like that because it gives patient observation a job. A player who pauses to consider an unusual detail is doing something useful, even when they are not moving towards the next obvious marker. Curiosity can be a form of progress without every moment needing a visible reward.

There is still a preference to recognise. Some players enjoy a clear sequence of short stages more than a space with several possible distractions. I would choose Super Mario Bros. Wonder for someone who mainly wants that side-view structure. Odyssey is the option I would favour when deciding what to investigate is part of the pleasure.

## Assist Mode is an invitation to learn

Nintendo describes switching between Regular Mode and Assist Mode at any time. The latter adds support such as more health and guidance towards the next destination. I appreciate being able to change that decision during the adventure, instead of having an early setting define the whole purchase.
I would use assistance to make room for learning one thing at a time. A player might want to understand a new movement or a camera view without also worrying about every consequence of a mistake. Reducing one source of pressure can leave more attention for the part that is still unfamiliar.
